Have you heard of the **1.01 rule**? The idea is that growing just 1% every day compounds into an enormous difference over the long run. The inverse is just as true: slack off by 1% every day, and the gap widens far more than you'd expect.

## The math makes the gap unmistakable

Written as an equation, it looks like this:

- Compounding at 1.01 daily: `1.01^365 ≈ 37.8`
- Eroding at 0.99 daily: `0.99^365 ≈ 0.03`

The daily difference is barely visible, but stretched across a year, the picture changes completely. Small daily effort compounds into a large long-term outcome. I touched on this idea before in ["Why Study for Certifications When AI Exists?" — Returning to Basics](/HomePage/en/blog/20260617-back-to-basics-study/), but it's worth digging into on its own.

## What matters is the 0.01 gap, not a dramatic leap

What the 1.01 rule actually teaches is that **sustained small progress beats dramatic change.** Occasionally cramming ten hours of study in one sitting, psyching yourself up once a month, going all-out only when inspiration strikes — none of that beats studying a little every day, moving your hands a little every day, getting a little further than yesterday every day. Growth doesn't usually happen in a flashy jump. In practice, it happens far more often through unglamorous 0.01 increments.

## The comparison point is yesterday's you, not anyone else

Following this rule, I'm aiming for today's me to be 0.01 ahead of yesterday's me, and tomorrow's me to be 0.01 ahead of today's. The comparison point here is deliberately **yesterday's you, not other people.** Living by comparison to others tends to spiral: you see someone further ahead, panic, feel discouraged, and freeze up — a loop that makes consistency much harder. But "I read a little more than yesterday" or "I did that a little more carefully than yesterday" is realistic, and realistic things are easier to sustain.

## "Doing your best" isn't the same as perfectionism

Here's an important distinction: doing your best doesn't mean doing it perfectly. Chase perfection, and you start telling yourself "I don't have enough time today, so I'll skip it" or "this isn't polished enough to ship." Think that way for long enough, and the zero-days start piling up. What the 1.01 rule actually asks for is **doing your best within today's actual constraints.** Reading for five minutes on an exhausted day, jotting down a single line on a packed one — that's still 1.01. What matters isn't whether today was perfect. It's whether you moved 1% further than yesterday.

## Invisible day to day, unmistakable a year out

Here's what makes the 1.01 rule both demanding and interesting: **the difference is nearly invisible on any given day.** One good day doesn't transform you dramatically, and one bad day doesn't unravel you either. That's exactly why people get complacent — "today's fine to skip," "this gap is basically rounding error." But that small margin, compounded across 365 days, becomes a real gap. Precisely because today's action is small, it's not something to take lightly.

## Consistency isn't a personality trait — it's a system

I don't think people who keep growing are unusually willed or gritty. I think the ones who sustain it are the ones who are good at **building a system.** Study at the same time every day. Set a rule that the day is never a hard zero. Log what you learn. Publish it somewhere that holds you accountable. Systems like these make you less dependent on how you happen to feel that day. This is the same idea I explored in [Don't Bring Motivation to Work — Deliver Consistent Results Through Systems, Like Brushing Your Teeth](/HomePage/en/blog/20260423-motivation-is-not-needed/): results built on systems, not on willpower. The 1.01 rule isn't a pep talk — treat it as **a design principle for sustaining small effort**, and it becomes genuinely practical.

This kind of 0.01 accumulation doesn't stay confined to your own growth, either. As I wrote in ["No Effort Goes Unrewarded" — How the Concept of Accumulating Luck Changed My Work](/HomePage/en/blog/20260414-effort-and-luck/), the same daily 1% effort that grows you also feeds what you contribute to the people around you.

## Closing thoughts

My working principle is to do my best today. The 1.01 rule is what makes that principle practical: growing 1% a day compounds into a large gap a year out, and small daily differences accumulate into a changed landscape. What matters isn't perfection — it's whether you moved a little further than yesterday.

If you feel lately like you haven't grown much, or that you're not moving forward the way you'd hoped, start by paying attention to 1.01. You can't manufacture a big result overnight. But you can make small progress starting today. End today having done your best with what you had — that 0.01 is what becomes tomorrow's real difference.
