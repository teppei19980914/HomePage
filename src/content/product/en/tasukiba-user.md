## Do these sound familiar?

> "If we'd had that knowledge back then, the estimate could have been much sharper."
> "If the previous person had left a note, I wouldn't have burned half a day on the same incident."

On real projects, the same failures repeat themselves, and valuable judgment and lessons end up buried in someone's head — or scattered across documents nobody reads. The moment a person leaves, that knowledge goes with them.

**Tasukiba** starts from that frustration. We want to reduce it with a system, not with sheer willpower.

## What it solves

| Field problem | What Tasukiba does |
|---|---|
| Estimates have no reasoning, every time it's from scratch | Stores estimate hours, reasoning, and linked knowledge as history |
| Risks and countermeasures scatter; recurrence prevention fails | Links risk/issue records to knowledge so patterns don't repeat |
| Retrospectives become ritual, never feeding into the next project | A retrospective → knowledge → next project loop, by design |
| The team runs on individuals; when one leaves it collapses | Projects, tasks, and knowledge structured for continued reuse |
| Quality, cost, and schedule are not visible together | Plan and actuals visualized end-to-end |

## Key features

The features a project needs day-to-day, connected around a **central knowledge base**.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Project management** | State transitions from planning to retrospective |
| **Estimation** | Higher-accuracy estimates referencing past performance and knowledge |
| **WBS / Task management** | Hierarchical tasks with owners and progress updates |
| **Gantt chart** | Timeline view of schedule, progress, delays, milestones |
| **Risk / Issue management** | From filing to countermeasure logs, with CSV export |
| **Knowledge base** | Full-text searchable knowledge base, reusable across projects |
| **Retrospective** | KPT-style review automatically promoted into the knowledge base |
| **My Tasks** | Personal task list with quick progress updates |

### What "knowledge at the center" actually means

Many project management tools place task management and knowledge in separate modules. Tasukiba puts **knowledge at the center**, with estimates, tasks, risks, and retrospectives all bidirectionally linked to it.

Just by doing the everyday work, *"knowledge that will help someone on the next project"* quietly accumulates.

## Where it fits

| Situation | What you get from Tasukiba |
|---|---|
| **You want to apply knowledge across projects** | Full-text search and auto-recommendation surface past knowledge at the moment you need it |
| **Know-how disappears when people leave** | Structured knowledge and decision history slash handover cost |
| **You want sharper estimates** | Past actuals and retrospectives become evidence under your number |
| **The same kinds of risks keep happening** | Similar past risks and countermeasures surface by project attributes |
| **You need to handle operational data safely** | MFA, audit logs, role-based access — built to run in real operations |
